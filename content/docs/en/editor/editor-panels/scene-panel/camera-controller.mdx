Configure the initial camera view before sharing a project.
It is the only introduction an external reviewer gets.

A reviewer who receives a link has no briefing and no context.
Whatever loads on screen decides whether they grasp the scope in seconds or form an impression from the wrong angle.

No other setting in the Scene Panel affects the result as much.

## Field reference

| Field | Description | Recommended usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Controls | The navigation controller type used in the 3D scene. | Match the controller to the project type and the expected viewing behaviour. |
| Initial Camera Position | Where the camera sits when the scene opens. | Place it where viewers immediately understand the main scene context. |
| Initial Camera Rotation | Which direction the camera faces when the scene opens. | Aim at the most important area, model, or spatial relationship. |
| Initial Target LookAt | The point the camera is oriented toward. | Use with camera position to create a stable, readable opening view. |
| Camera FOV | Field of view, meaning how wide the camera sees, like a wide-angle or zoom lens. | Tune alongside the opening view to balance how much fits on screen against how focused it feels. |

## Set controller type

| Scenario | Suits | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Object and map scenes | Third person, orbiting from outside | People look at these from outside, circling them the way you turn an object over in your hands |
| Space scenes | First person, walking through at eye level | You understand a room by walking around inside it |

Project type sets a sensible default at creation without locking it. Change it when a scene's actual use diverges from its category:

- A large object viewers need to walk around inside
- An interior presented as a cutaway rather than a walkthrough

## Set initial camera view

Three parameters work together:

- **Position** places the camera
- **Rotation** and **Target LookAt** decide what it addresses

A useful test: reload the scene and look at the first frame as though you had never seen the project.
If it takes more than a moment to work out what the scene contains and where the subject is, the opening view needs work.

## Set controller FOV

- **Wider view**, like a wide-angle lens: fits more of the surroundings on screen and makes distances look bigger.
  Good when the overall layout matters more than the detail.
- **Narrower view**, like zooming in: focuses on one thing and keeps its shape true. Good for showing a single object without distortion.

Review FOV together with the initial view rather than on its own.
A well-placed camera at the wrong FOV still produces visible distortion or an overly confined result.

## Why this matters

The reconstruction can be flawless and the assets perfectly placed.
A poor opening view still leaves a reviewer uncertain what they are being shown.

It takes a minute to correct and is often missed, because the person configuring it already knows the scene well.
