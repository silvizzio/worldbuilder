Camera controller settings define two things: how a viewer moves through the scene,
and what the scene shows when it first opens.

Both matter most for projects you send to someone outside the team.
A reviewer opening your link has no prior knowledge of the scene.
If the opening view starts inside a wall or points at empty ground, they have to navigate before they can tell what the project is.

Configure the opening view before you share the project.

## Field reference

| Field | What it does | How to set it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Controls | Chooses how the viewer moves: orbiting the scene from outside, or walking through it at eye level. | Match it to the kind of scene. Details in the next section. |
| Initial Camera Position | The point in the scene where the camera sits when the project opens. | Place it far enough back that the main subject is fully in frame. |
| Initial Camera Rotation | The direction the camera faces when the project opens. | Point it at the model or area the project is about. |
| Initial Target LookAt | The point the camera looks toward. Setting it keeps the camera aimed at that point. | Use it with the position above to frame the subject in the centre of the view. |
| Camera FOV | Field of view, measured in degrees. It controls how much of the scene fits on screen. | Start around 50 to 60 degrees. Adjust from there while watching the opening view. |

## Set controller type

| Scene | Controller | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Object and map scenes | Third person, orbiting from outside | The subject sits in front of the viewer, who rotates around it to see other sides |
| Space scenes | First person, walking at eye level | The viewer is inside the space and moves through it, as they would in the real building |

The project type you selected at creation sets one of these as the default.
Change it when the scene is used differently from its type.
An object large enough to walk inside is better served by the first-person controller,
and a room presented as a cutaway rather than a walkthrough is better served by the third-person one.

## Set initial camera view

Three fields together define the opening view:

- **Initial Camera Position** places the camera in the scene
- **Initial Camera Rotation** turns it to face a direction
- **Initial Target LookAt** fixes the point it aims at

Set the position first, then use the rotation or the look-at point to frame the subject.

To check it, reload the project and look only at the first frame.
If you cannot tell what the scene contains and where the main subject is, adjust it again.

## Set controller FOV

Field of view controls how much of the scene fits on screen at once.

- **A higher value**, around 75 to 90 degrees, fits more of the surroundings into the frame.
  Objects near the edges stretch, and the scene looks deeper than it is.
  Use it when the viewer needs to see the layout around the subject.
- **A lower value**, around 35 to 45 degrees, fits less into the frame and keeps proportions accurate.
  Use it when the viewer needs to look closely at one model.

Adjust the field of view while watching the opening view, not on its own.
A camera in the right place can still produce a distorted or cramped first frame if the field of view is wrong.

## Why this matters

A scan can be accurate and every model correctly placed, and the project still fails to communicate if it opens on the wrong view.

This is quick to fix and easy to overlook.
By the time you set it, you already know the scene, so a confusing opening view does not look confusing to you.
