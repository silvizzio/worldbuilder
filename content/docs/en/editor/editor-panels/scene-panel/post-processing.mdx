Use post-processing last, once lighting is correct.
It adjusts the rendered image, not the scene.

If assets are hard to make out, the cause is almost always lighting.
Adjusting the image instead treats the symptom.

Use this module once the scene, camera, and model placement are stable.


## Where it sits in the workflow

| Layer | Works on | Fix problems here when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Lighting](/en/docs/editor/editor-panels/scene-panel/lighting) | The 3D scene itself | Assets appear flat, dark, or lack form |
| Post-processing | The rendered image | The scene is correct but the output lacks polish |

## When to use it

- **Preparing for external review**: improves visual consistency before a reviewer sees it
- **The scene still appears flat after lighting is correct**: adjusts image-level contrast and balance that lighting alone does not address
- **Presenting across devices**: reduces variation under different viewing conditions
- **Producing for demonstration or marketing**: adds the finish that separates a working scene from a presentable one

## Field reference

Post-processing is enabled by a toggle at the top of the section. Once on, the controls are grouped by effect.

| Group | Field | What it controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General | Render Scale | How much detail the scene is drawn at. Higher looks sharper and runs slower. |
| General | Tone Mapping | How bright and dark areas are balanced across the whole picture. |
| Bloom | Bloom Intensity | How much bright areas glow, like a light source blooming on camera. |
| Bloom | Bloom Blur Level | How far the glow spreads from its source. |
| Color Grading | Saturation | Colour intensity across the image. |
| Color Grading | Brightness | Overall image brightness. |
| Color Grading | Contrast | The difference between light and dark areas. |
| Vignette | Vignette Intensity | How much the corners of the picture darken, drawing the eye to the centre. |
| Vignette | Vignette Inner | Where the vignette begins from the centre outward. |
| Vignette | Vignette Outer | Where the vignette reaches full strength. |
| Fringing | Fringing Intensity | Coloured fringes along sharp edges, the effect a real camera lens produces. |
| TAA | Enable TAA | Smooths jagged edges on diagonal lines. TAA is short for temporal anti-aliasing. |
| TAA | TAA Jitter | How hard the edge smoothing works. |

## Configure post-processing effects

Enable post-processing, then adjust the image parameters to suit the presentation and review context.

Review under the same viewing conditions the audience will use.
A scene tuned on a bright colour-accurate display can look different on a laptop in a meeting room.
If the project is headed for a customer presentation, check it on something close to what will be in the room.

## A note on restraint

Adjustments that look striking while tuning often read as heavy-handed when a viewer sees the scene for the first time.

The test is simple: does a viewer notice the effect, or notice the content? For inspection, review, or explanation work, the content should win.

## Why it matters

The same reconstruction, at the same accuracy, is received differently depending on whether the output looks finished or provisional.
That judgement gets made in the first few seconds, by people who are not evaluating the reconstruction on its technical merits.
