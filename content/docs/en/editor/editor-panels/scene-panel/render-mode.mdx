**Render Mode**, under **Base Mode** in the Scene Panel, determines whether scene lights affect the model or it retains the appearance captured in its textures.

<ShotGrid images={['/demos/project-center/screens/render-lit.png', '/demos/project-center/screens/render-unlit.png']} />

## The two modes

| Mode | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Lit | Scene lighting affects the model. Ambient, sky, and key light all change what you see. |
| Unlit | Scene lighting is ignored. The model shows the textures and colours it was captured with. |

## Why Unlit exists

A scanned model already contains light. When a site is photographed for
reconstruction, the sun, the sky, and every lamp in the room are recorded into
the surface colours. That is called **baked lighting**, meaning the light is part
of the texture rather than something calculated as you view it.

Adding scene lighting over baked lighting can create overlapping shadows and highlights that were not present in the scan. If the result looks muddy, compare it with Unlit before adjusting the lights further.

The panel says as much: Unlit suits scanned or baked-texture assets.

## Choosing between them

Use **Unlit** when the model came from a scan and already looks right. The
lighting controls below will do nothing, which is expected.

Use **Lit** when you want control over presentation, or when the model was built
without baked lighting, as most manually authored assets are. Then the settings
in [Lighting](/worldbuilder/en/docs/step-by-step-workflow/6-lighting) take effect.

If you are unsure, switch between them and watch the model. The difference is
immediate, and whichever reads more clearly is the right answer for that asset.
