title: Lighting
description: Configure render mode, ambient light, sky light, and key light to control how scene illumination presents 3D assets during review and sharing.

Lighting controls how scene illumination affects 3D asset presentation. Switch the render mode, adjust ambient light, enable sky light, and configure key light direction and intensity to improve how models read in the viewport.

Lighting is the difference between an asset a viewer can inspect and one they can only look at. Material detail, surface texture, and the depth cues that let someone judge form all depend on how light falls. A reconstruction captured at high fidelity can still present as a flat silhouette if it is lit badly.

Configure lighting after the main model assets are loaded and before the project enters final preview or publishing.

## Field reference

| Field | Description | Recommended usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Render Mode | Whether the scene uses lighting-based rendering or shows mesh materials by their original textures and colours. | Use **Lit** when scene lighting should affect presentation. Use **Unlit** for scanned or baked-texture assets that should keep their original appearance. |
| Ambient Color | The base ambient light colour applied to the scene. | Use a neutral colour for clean product or asset presentation. |
| Ambient Intensity | The strength of ambient light. | Increase to brighten overall model visibility; reduce when the scene needs stronger directional contrast. |
| Sky Light | Enables or disables sky light contribution. | Enable when the scene needs softer environmental illumination. |
| Sky Light Color | The colour of the sky light. | Use subtle cool or neutral tones so broad illumination does not overpower model materials. |
| Sky Light Intensity | The strength of sky light. | Adjust together with ambient light to balance overall brightness. |
| Key Light | Enables or disables the main key light. | Enable when the asset needs a stronger primary light direction for shape and surface definition. |
| Key Light Color | The colour of the key light. | Use neutral or warm tones when presenting objects or products with natural material highlights. |
| Key Light Intensity | The strength of the key light. | Increase to emphasise form and material detail; reduce if highlights become too strong. |
| Key Light Yaw | The horizontal direction of the key light. | Rotate the main light around the asset to avoid unwanted shadow direction. |
| Key Light Pitch | The vertical direction of the key light. | Control whether light arrives from a higher or lower angle. |

## The three light sources

Scene illumination is built from three contributions, and understanding what each does makes tuning them far quicker than adjusting values at random.

| Source | Contributes | Without it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ambient | An even base light across the whole scene | Shadowed areas go unreadably dark |
| Sky light | Broad, soft environmental illumination | Contrast becomes harsh, surfaces read as stark |
| Key light | A single directional source with a definable angle | Form flattens, surfaces lose their sense of shape |

Most scenes need all three in balance. Ambient establishes visibility, sky light softens, and key light restores the directional definition that makes an object look three-dimensional.

## Configure render mode

Use **Render Mode** to switch how the scene handles lighting. **Lit** applies scene lighting to the model, making ambient, sky, and key light settings visible in the viewport. **Unlit** disables scene lighting and shows mesh materials by their original textures or colours.

The choice depends on what the asset already contains. A scanned asset often carries lighting information baked into its texture from the moment of capture. Adding scene lighting on top of baked lighting produces a doubled, muddy result, which is why Unlit frequently looks better for reconstruction output.

When the project needs controlled visual presentation, or when assets were produced without baked lighting, Lit gives far more flexibility.

## Set ambient light

Use **Ambient Color** and ambient intensity to define the base light level. Ambient light governs overall visibility and prevents dark areas from becoming unreadable.

For object-level assets, moderate ambient intensity improves surface visibility without flattening the model. Pushing it too high is the most common lighting mistake: the scene brightens, but form disappears because nothing casts a discernible shadow any more.

For space or map scenes, balance ambient against sky and key light so the scene keeps depth and contrast.

## Enable sky light

Use the **Sky Light** switch to enable or disable environmental illumination. Sky light adds broader, softer light and reduces harsh contrast when a model needs more balanced presentation.

Adjust **Sky Light Color** and intensity together. Subtle cool or neutral tones support broad illumination without tinting the asset. Excessive intensity flattens the sense of depth in the same way excessive ambient does.

## Configure key light

Use the **Key Light** switch to enable or disable the primary light source. Key light is what gives an asset shape definition, visible surface detail, and a clear presentation direction.

Adjust **Key Light Color**, intensity, yaw, and pitch together. Yaw moves the light horizontally around the asset; pitch changes its vertical angle. Between them they determine how highlights and shadows fall, which is what a viewer reads as form.

A practical starting point is to place the key light off to one side rather than head on. Light arriving from directly in front of the viewer eliminates the shadows that describe shape.

## Review guidance

After configuring lighting, review the scene through the same camera view intended for preview or sharing. Lighting that works from an inspection angle can fail from the opening view.

Lighting should support the content without hiding detail, overexposing surfaces, or casting distracting shadows. If a viewer would struggle to judge material or form, the lighting is not finished regardless of how the numbers look.

If the project uses scanned or baked-texture assets, compare Lit and Unlit before finalising. Which is better depends on whether scene lighting improves the asset or fights its captured appearance, and that varies asset by asset.
