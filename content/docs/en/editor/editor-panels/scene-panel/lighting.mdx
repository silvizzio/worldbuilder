---
title: Lighting
description: Configure render mode, ambient light, sky light, and key light to control how scene illumination presents 3D assets during review and sharing.
---
Lighting settings control how the scene is illuminated, which determines how much of a model a viewer can actually see.

Capture accuracy alone does not decide this.
A model scanned in high detail can still appear as a flat shape on screen if it is lit poorly,
because surface texture, material, and depth all depend on how light falls across it.

Configure lighting after the main models are loaded, and before the project goes to preview or publishing.

## Field reference

| Field | Description | Recommended usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Render Mode | Whether the scene uses lighting-based rendering or shows mesh materials by their original textures and colours. | Use **Lit** when scene lighting should affect presentation. Use **Unlit** for scanned or baked-texture assets that should keep their original appearance. |
| Ambient Color | The base ambient light colour applied to the scene. | Use a neutral colour for clean product or asset presentation. |
| Ambient Intensity | The strength of ambient light. | Increase to brighten overall model visibility. Reduce when the scene needs stronger directional contrast. |
| Sky Light | Enables or disables sky light contribution. | Enable when the scene needs softer environmental illumination. |
| Sky Light Color | The colour of the sky light. | Use subtle cool or neutral tones so broad illumination does not overpower model materials. |
| Sky Light Intensity | The strength of sky light. | Adjust together with ambient light to balance overall brightness. |
| Key Light | Enables or disables the main key light. | Enable when the asset needs a stronger primary light direction for shape and surface definition. |
| Key Light Color | The colour of the key light. | Use neutral or warm tones when presenting objects or products with natural material highlights. |
| Key Light Intensity | The strength of the key light. | Increase to emphasise form and material detail. Reduce if highlights become too strong. |
| Key Light Yaw | The horizontal direction of the key light. | Rotate the main light around the asset to avoid unwanted shadow direction. |
| Key Light Pitch | The vertical direction of the key light. | Control whether light arrives from a higher or lower angle. |

## The three light sources

| Light | What it does | Without it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ambient | An even glow everywhere, like an overcast day | Shadowed areas go too dark to see |
| Sky light | Soft light from above, as if from open sky | Contrast turns harsh and surfaces look stark |
| Key light | One main light from an angle you choose, like a lamp | Shapes flatten out and lose their form |

Most scenes need all three in balance:

- **Ambient** establishes visibility
- **Sky light** softens
- **Key light** restores the directional definition that makes an object appear three-dimensional

## Configure render mode

- **Lit**: the scene lights actually affect the model, so your lighting settings change what you see
- **Unlit**: the lights are ignored and the model shows the colours and textures it was captured with

The choice depends on what the model already has in it.
A scanned model usually has the real-world lighting from the day of capture already recorded in its surface.
Add more light on top of that and you get two sets of shadows fighting each other, which looks muddy.
That is why Unlit frequently looks better for reconstruction output.

When the project needs controlled visual presentation, or the assets were produced without baked lighting, Lit offers considerably more flexibility.

## Set ambient light

Ambient light governs overall visibility and prevents dark areas becoming impossible to make out.

- **Object-level assets**: moderate intensity improves surface visibility without flattening the model
- **Space or map scenes**: balance ambient against sky and key light so the scene retains depth

Pushing ambient too high is the most common lighting mistake. The scene brightens, but form disappears because nothing casts a discernible shadow any longer.

## Enable sky light

Sky light adds broader, softer light and reduces harsh contrast when a model needs more balanced presentation.

Adjust colour and intensity together.
Subtle cool or neutral tones support broad illumination without tinting the asset.
Excessive intensity flattens depth the same way excessive ambient does.

## Configure key light

Key light gives an asset shape definition, visible surface detail, and a clear presentation direction.

Four settings work together:

- **Colour**: neutral or slightly warm looks most natural on materials
- **Intensity**: how bright the light is, which controls how much shape and texture you see
- **Yaw**: swings the light left and right around the model
- **Pitch**: raises or lowers it, from overhead to closer to eye level

A practical starting point is to place the key light off to one side rather than head on.
Light arriving from directly in front of the viewer eliminates the shadows that describe shape.

## Review guidance

- Review through the same camera view intended for preview or sharing. Lighting that works from an inspection angle can fail from the opening view.
- Check that nothing is hidden, overexposed, or casting distracting shadows.
  If a viewer would struggle to judge material or form, the lighting is not finished regardless of how the numbers look.
- For scanned or baked-texture assets, compare Lit and Unlit before finalising. Which is better varies asset by asset.
