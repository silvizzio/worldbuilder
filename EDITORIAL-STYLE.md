# Documentation writing style

Write clear, natural English that helps readers understand the product and complete a task. Begin with the feature’s purpose and its place in the workflow, then explain the action and expected result.

Use complete sentences and connected paragraphs for explanations. Keep labels and tables concise. Avoid slogans, repeated claims, unnecessary adjectives, and strings of technical nouns. Prefer familiar words, and explain specialist terms when the audience may not know them.

Add detail when it answers a practical question: where to start, what a control changes, why a setting matters, or how to interpret a result. Use examples supported by the product. Do not invent capabilities, values, data sources, or guarantees.

Keep interface labels, units, identifiers, links, and technical distinctions accurate. Distinguish current behaviour from proposals, predictions, and incomplete specifications. A clearer sentence must not change the underlying fact.

Use numbered steps for ordered actions and tables for comparisons or reference values. Captions should explain what the screenshot shows and how it relates to the surrounding text.

Before publishing, read the page from a new user’s perspective. Check grammar and flow, compare the instructions with the product evidence, and preview the rendered page. Preserve working code examples and validate MDX after editing.

For example, write “Select a detection to open its details and compare its outline with the satellite image.” This tells the reader what to do and why.
